# Glossary

Use this page to record terms and ideas that help you understand
professional analytics projects.

This project covers BI storytelling:
choosing one business goal and producing a clear, actionable recommendation.

Pro-tip: Expand the VS Code **Outline** view (below the navigator on the right)
to see this file organization at-a-glance.

## BI Storytelling

### business goal

A business goal is a specific outcome the organization wants to achieve.
In BI, the business goal drives every decision about data, metrics, and analysis.
A well-defined goal produces actionable insights; a vague goal produces noise.

### business question

A business question is a precise, answerable question that supports a business goal.
For example: "Which product category generates the most revenue in the West region?"
A good business question has a clear answer that suggests a specific action.

### business recommendation

A business recommendation is a specific action suggested by the analysis.
It connects the data finding to something the business can actually do.
A BI analysis that ends without a recommendation is incomplete.

### narrative

A narrative is the written story that explains what the data shows and why it matters.
Good BI narratives connect the analysis to the business goal,
explain the finding in plain language, and state the recommendation clearly.

### audience

The audience for a BI report is the person or team who will act on the findings.
Writing for the audience means using their vocabulary,
answering their questions, and framing findings in terms they care about.

### heatmap

A heatmap displays values in a matrix using color intensity.
Darker or more saturated colors represent higher values.
It is useful for comparing performance across two dimensions at once,
such as product category by region.

## Analysis Concepts

### revenue

Revenue is the total money received from sales before any costs are deducted.
It is one of the most common BI metrics because it directly measures business performance.
Total revenue by category or region is a foundational BI analysis.

### trend

A trend is a general direction that data moves over time.
An upward trend in monthly sales suggests growth.
A downward trend suggests decline and may warrant investigation.

### segmentation

Segmentation divides customers, products, or sales into groups
based on shared characteristics.
BI analysts use segmentation to identify high-value groups
and tailor actions to each segment.

### benchmark

A benchmark is a reference value used for comparison.
For example, comparing a region's sales to the company average
provides context for whether performance is strong or weak.

### outlier

An outlier is a data point that is unusually far from the others.
In a sales context, an unusually large or small transaction may be an error
or may represent a genuinely unusual event worth investigating.

## Documentation

### docs/index.md

`docs/index.md` is the main narrative page for your project documentation.
It is where you write your business goal, analysis approach, findings,
and recommendation in full sentences.

### README.md

`README.md` is the landing page for your GitHub repository.
It describes the project, lists commands, and links to executed code.
The README is often the first thing a reader sees.

### markdown

Markdown is a lightweight text formatting language.
Headers start with `#`, bold text uses `**`, and links use `[text](url)`.
Markdown files render as formatted pages on GitHub and in zensical docs.
